Method

How this was made

All seven TikToks were downloaded directly; the five Instagram Reels required routing through an embed-proxy to capture the CDN stream (Instagram blocks anonymous API access). Each video was then sampled into 12–24 evenly spaced frames with ffmpeg and read frame-by-frame, alongside its caption, hashtags, and engagement metadata. The strips you see in each dossier are six of those sampled frames, in chronological order — a compressed storyboard of the actual video.

Every video here is a beauty product video — foundation, lipstick, blush, eyeshadow, and one vanity mirror — which makes the set unusually coherent: these are twelve different answers to the same question, “how do you make a person stop scrolling for a cosmetic?”

Synthesis

The common DNA

Across six formats, two platforms, and a 60× spread in production budget, the same seven mechanics recur. These are the features to engineer into any generated video:

1 · The hook is physical, not verbal

Every video earns its first two seconds with something in motion toward the camera: a compact snapping open (8, 12), a bullet of glitter filling the lens (5), a contrarian sentence already mid-flow (1), a sticker on a lip (10). None open with a logo, a title card, or an establishing shot. For generated video this means: the first frame should already be the interesting frame.

2 · Packaging is a character

Seven of twelve videos treat the product’s object design — embossing, carving, gems, sculpted lids — as the protagonist. C-beauty brands (Flower Knows, Florasis, Judydoll, Cheeryep, Joocyee) win here because their packaging is literally sculptural. The camera language is jewelry-commercial: macro distance, slow rotation, raking light that keeps a glint moving across relief surfaces.

3 · Proof happens on skin, on camera

Whenever a claim exists, it is tested visibly: half-face application (1), arm swatches (4, 12), the water-and-tissue gauntlet (11), shade-by-shade try-on (5, 10). The audience trusts the pixels, not the voiceover. Generated content must simulate this evidentiary beat to feel native.

4 · Contrast architecture

The highest-engagement videos are built on a hard cut between two worlds: dim bedroom → purple dreamscape (4), bonnet skepticism → douyin doll (6), bare lip → deep red (10). One cut, placed on a musical drop, carrying the entire promise of the product. The “before” must be genuinely unglamorous — its honesty is what makes the “after” believable.

5 · Serial rhythm trains retention

Multi-item videos (5’s four shades, 8’s six products, 2’s two brands) repeat an identical beat with new content, like a slot machine: reveal → texture → reaction → next. Viewers stay because the pattern promises one more payoff, and the algorithm reads that retention as quality.

6 · A face that overreacts

Where a face appears, it performs at 130%: bug eyes, pouts, deadpan skepticism, knowing glances. The face is a subtitle track for emotion — legible at thumbnail size, meme-able as a still.

7 · Comments are designed, not hoped for

“Which one wins?” (2), an inspo photo to grade (6), a personified mirror to covet (3), a hated category redeemed (1) — each video plants a specific argument for the comment section. Shares and saves follow the formats that give viewers a job: judge, covet, learn, or disagree.

The one-line formula: a tactile hook in frame one → an ornate object or honest face as protagonist → visible proof on skin → one hard contrast cut on the beat → an ending that hands the viewer something to say, save, or covet.
The deliverable

The prompt playbook

Six prompt kits, one per archetype. Replace the bracketed tokens with your own assets:

  • [CHARACTER] — your character description, kept identical across every prompt for consistency (e.g. “a 22-year-old East-Asian woman with jet-black waist-length hair, porcelain skin, soft doll makeup, long pearl-tipped nails” — or your brand mascot).
  • [PRODUCT] — your product with its distinctive physical details (e.g. “a blush compact shaped like a satin pillow in metallic rose-gold with an embossed camellia lid”). The more sculptural detail you write, the more the video sells it.
Workflow that works: generate image-first. Compose a first-frame still (your real product photo composited into the scene, or a generated frame from the image prompt in each kit), then feed it to Seedance image-to-video or Grok Imagine i2v with the motion prompt. This locks product fidelity — text-to-video will hallucinate your packaging. Always 9:16 vertical. Generate clips of 5–10s (Seedance) or ~6s (Grok Imagine), then stitch, add text overlays and trending audio in your editor — never ask the model to render on-screen text. For Seedance, camera grammar (“handheld selfie framing”, “macro dolly-in”, “rack focus”) is followed closely; Grok Imagine responds best to one clear motion + one emotion per clip.

Kit A · The Honest Review

Modeled on videos 1 & 11 — trust through live, uncut proof.

First-frame image prompt
Vertical 9:16 smartphone selfie photo, [CHARACTER] holding [PRODUCT] up beside their face at arm's length, mid-sentence expression with raised eyebrow, warm natural window light from the left, slightly cluttered real bedroom softly blurred behind, honest unretouched skin texture, phone-camera realism, no studio lighting.
Seedance video prompt (10s, multi-shot)
Handheld vertical selfie video, phone-camera realism. Shot 1: [CHARACTER] speaks directly to camera with a skeptical frown, holding [PRODUCT] beside their face, warm window light, real bedroom background. Shot 2: cut to closer framing — they open [PRODUCT] on camera and apply it to one cheek with its applicator, leaning toward the lens so the skin texture is visible. Shot 3: they turn their face to show the finished side, eyebrows lifting from skepticism into pleasant surprise, small smile. Natural micro hand-shake throughout, authentic vlog energy, no color grading.
Grok Imagine clip beats (~6s each, from first-frame image)
Clip 1: she talks animatedly to camera, skeptical expression, gesturing with the product; handheld wobble.
Clip 2: she applies the product to her cheek while leaning into the lens, concentrating; camera drifts closer.
Clip 3: she inspects her cheek in the light, breaks into a surprised, convinced smile, nods at camera.
Post: overlay a contrarian opening line as text ("I really wanted to hate this...") in the first second; add the transfer-test beat (tissue rub on the back of the hand) as an optional Clip 4 for claims.
